# P6.22 Where a field frame's CPU time goes

Measured 2026-10-24 on the PC build, as a proxy for the Switch. Goal of
P6.22 ([ROADMAP](ROADMAP.md#phase-6-goal-notes)): turbo at 4x reaches only
about 68 game fps in the field and scripted scenes on the OLED (rc7 log,
about 13.7 ms of CPU per game frame), against about 190 in battle.

## Result

- **The CPU rasterizer is about 97 % of a field frame.** It is PSY-Z's
  `sdl3_software.c`, which the Switch draws everything outside battle with
  (`switch_gpu.c`). In the field and the scripted scenes of the New Game
  opening it costs 2.2-2.6 ms per game frame on the PC, against about 60-70 us
  for all the game logic, the OT walk and the pad and sound code together.
- **Most of that is rectangles.** The map is drawn as 128x128 8-bit CLUT
  sprites, plus a full-screen clear (a 320x240 TILE, wider with the gutters).
  Textured triangles (the dialogue window and text) are about 10 %.
- **Top against bottom of the Forest Inn is fill, not logic.** The same 29
  map sprites and 47-48 primitives are drawn at both spots, but at the top the
  camera shows more of the map: 184 000 sprite pixels per frame against
  142 000 at the bottom, where a large part of the screen is the black void
  below the floor that no map block covers. +29 % pixels gives +29 % raster
  time; the game logic is the same (66 against 63 us).
- **Implemented:** a faster CPU rasterizer in PSY-Z (`dw3-soft-raster-fast`,
  `2f931d8`), same pixels: 4.0-5.4x less raster time in field, menu and
  scripted scenes, 0 differing pixels in 154 605 frames of 18 US and EU
  replays and in 6 million random operations over the full GP0 ranges.
- **Pass 3 (2026-10-28):** textured triangles about 1.6x faster (same pixels),
  the mixer thread off the game thread's core, and a `pace dbg:` line that
  splits every 60-frame window; see [Pass 3](#pass-3-2026-10-28-textured-polygons-the-mixers-core-and-the-pace-line).
  The OLED has still not reported `frame dt us=` for the field.
- **Pass 4 (2026-10-30):** the present uploads the VRAM halfwords (`R16UI`) and the
  shader expands them, instead of the CPU packing RGBA8888: same pixels (all 65 536
  values and five scales on Mesa, 11 274 shown frames byte-compared in Ryubing),
  the CPU side of a shown frame about 40-60 % lower in Ryubing; see
  [Pass 4](#pass-4-2026-10-30-the-present-uploads-16-bit-vram). The OLED A/B is
  OLED_TEST_PLAN C11.
- **Expected on the OLED:** the field at 4x from about 68 game fps to roughly
  160-240 (the 4x target is 240 US, 200 EU). This is an estimate from the PC
  (below), not a measurement; the OLED check is open.

## Pass 2 (2026-10-25): frames turbo does not show are not drawn

After #220 the CPU rasterizer was still 86-93 % of a field frame in the
opening (profiler build, US, 4x: 0.7-1.2 ms of a 1.0-1.6 ms game frame on the
PC). Turbo presents one game frame in four, but the Switch drew all four.
Pass 2 stops drawing the three nobody sees, exactly.

**How.** PSY-Z `dw3-soft-defer` (`sdl3_software_defer.inc`, see
[PSYZ.md](PSYZ.md#deferred-drawing-p622-pass-2)). While turbo skips presents the
rasterizer queues primitives in layers (one per drawing area) with their
drawing state and a footprint: the VRAM rectangle and gutter rows they can
write, and the VRAM they can read. A layer is dropped when a later opaque
rectangle (the clear that starts every field frame) or an image op overwrites
all of it before anything looked at it. It is drawn, with the older layers it
depends on, when something can see it: the present (`SyncRect` of the display
rectangle), `StoreImage`, `MoveImage` (the encounter transition's readback),
`LoadImage`/`ClearImage` over its destination, a gutter clear over its rows, or
`GetVram`. An image op that rewrites textures a layer samples (the actor frames
and CLUT rows loaded every few frames) keeps the old pixels for the layer
instead of drawing it. `SetGutter` is a no-op when nothing changes (the flip
called it every frame, which would have drawn everything). Battle keeps the
immediate path. The game logic is untouched.

**Exactness.** Footprints are supersets; `tools/soft_raster_bench.sh --check`
draws every queued primitive twice (it may only change pixels inside its
predicted rectangle, and must give the same pixels with everything outside
its predicted sources replaced by noise).

## Pass 3 (2026-10-28): textured polygons, the mixer's core, and the pace line

The owner's OLED test of preview.41 (plan item C1, 2026-10-27) says turbo works
in the intro, but sent no `frame dt` lines, so there is still no measured game
fps on the console. This pass (1) re-measured on the PC where a scripted-scene
frame goes now that pass 2 is in, (2) took the two things that measurement
still points at and that cannot hurt a pixel, and (3) made the Switch log say
where a window's time goes, so the next round needs no guessing.

**Where the time is now (PC, profiler build, `DW3_TURBO=4`, deferral on, mean
and median of 60-frame windows, US).** The PC numbers are the Switch-relevant
part only (rasterizer plus game logic); the OLED is about 5.3x this PC (rc7
calibration above).

| Scene | Raster per game frame (p50) | Game logic (p50) | Estimated OLED game frame | Share of the 4.17 ms (240 fps) budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `us-newgame-field`, pass 2 only | 179 us | 76 us | 255 us x 5.3 + 0.7 ms present = 2.0 ms | about 48 % |
| `us-text-opening` (dialogue, long scripted text), pass 2 only | 342 us | 89 us | 431 us x 5.3 + 0.7 = 3.0 ms | about 72 % |

So with pass 2 the New Game opening should be at the 4x cap and the dialogue
scenes close to it; this is an estimate, not a reading. The costs that were
left, by size: the CPU rasterizer at the presented frames (sprites 60-65 %,
textured polygons 30-65 %), the present (about 0.7 ms per game frame on the
OLED, rc7), the game logic (about 0.4 ms), the mixer. A trace of the text
scenes shows why the polygons cost more than their area suggests: 128 triangles
per frame (about 40 sheared 12-16 px quads, shadows and effects, and 3 scaled
40x40 quads), 32 rows and 262 plotted pixels per triangle, 18 200 cycles per
triangle (rdtsc, PC): 70 per plotted pixel. Taking the scan from the left of the
bounding box to the first covered column out of every row gave 15 800; the
dither shortcut (the game draws with dither on at the neutral color, so
`ShadeTexel`'s arithmetic ran for every texel) and the row steps without
divisions (5 per row: two attributes, three edges) gave 11 200.

**What changed**

- PSY-Z `dw3-soft-tri-fast` (`192c006`, one commit on `3208f31`, only
  `sdl3_software.c`): a row's covered columns come from the three edge
  functions (each bounds the column offset from one side, floor and ceiling
  division in integers) instead of a scan; those bounds and the start values of
  the interpolated attributes follow the rows with an add and a carry instead of
  a division (`SpanEdge`, `RowLerpNext`; a start that moved more than 3 columns,
  or a skipped row, divides as before); a flat textured triangle at the neutral
  color with dither on shades a texel by a per-channel decrement on the dither
  cells with d < 0 (`DitherNeutralTexel`), which is what `(8 t + d) >> 3` is for
  d in -4..3. Pixels, their order and their values are unchanged.
- `platforms/switch/psyz_backend/nx_audio.c`: the SPU mixer thread is created on
  **core 1** (it was `cpuid -2`, the game thread's core, at a higher priority,
  so its 4-8 % of a core was taken from the game thread; the map-view workers
  already run on core 2). `DW3_NX_AUDIO_CORE=-2` in `DW3_NX_TEST_ENV` restores
  the old placement for an A/B; if core 1 refuses, it falls back to `-2`. The
  log says which: `nx_audio: mixer thread on core 1 (prio 0x20)`.
- **`pace dbg:` line** (`nx_gpu.c`, `dw3_nx_gpu_pace_log`, printed with every
  `frame dt us=` line): `speed`, `game_fps`, `frames`, `presented` and
  `skipped`, and the window's time split into `sync_us` (the CPU rasterizer
  drawing what the shown frames need), `present_us` (pack, upload, draw),
  `swap_us` (inside `eglSwapBuffers`, the wait for the display), `other_us`
  (game logic, queueing the skipped frames, the vsync pump, waits on the audio
  lock), `busy_pct` (100 x (dt - swap) / dt) and `mix_us` (the mixer thread's CPU).
  Counted per present, a few tick reads; no cost per pixel. How to read it: at
  turbo, `game_fps` at the cap and `swap_us` large = the display is the limit
  (done); `game_fps` under the cap with `busy_pct` near 100 = CPU bound, and
  `sync_us`, `present_us` and `other_us` say which part. The first line after
  boot also counts the frames before the first full window and reads high.

## Pass 4 (2026-10-30): the present uploads 16-bit VRAM

Option 3 of the table below: the Switch present packed 102 000 pixels of RGB555
into RGBA8888 on the CPU (`nx_vram_pack.c`, a 64K-entry table, one load and one
store per pixel) and uploaded 4 bytes a pixel on every shown frame. It now
uploads the VRAM halfwords as they are, in a `GL_R16UI` texture, and the
fragment shader does the expansion. Nothing else in the present changed: same
rectangles, same NEAREST sampling, same viewport maths, same battle-mesh overlay
rule. **The change is in the port's present code (`platforms/switch/`); the
PSY-Z fork (`Larbaco/psyz`) is not touched and nothing was pushed to it.**

**What changed**

- `platforms/switch/psyz_backend/nx_present16.h` (new): the `#version 300 es`
  fragment shaders for the opaque blit and for the battle overlay, and the
  upload helpers. `(c5 * 255 + 15) / 31` in integers, the same function as
  `color_5to8`; the overlay's "all-black pixel is dropped unless bit 15 is set
  inside the partner view" rule becomes `(v & 0x7FFF) == 0` and `v & 0x8000`,
  which is what `c.r + c.g + c.b < 0.002` and `c.a > 0.5` meant for the RGBA
  bytes. The coordinate arithmetic keeps the old shaders' `mediump float`, so a
  driver that runs mediump in fp16 picks the same texel as before (see the
  llvmpipe finding below); only the colour work is `highp` integer.
- `nx_gpu.c`: `present_build(p16)` makes either set of programs and textures;
  `present_init` takes the 16-bit set on an ES 3 context unless
  `DW3_PRESENT_16=0`, and falls back to the RGBA8888 set (logging why) if the
  shaders do not link or an `R16UI` texture is refused. The gutter rectangle
  (P6.1) is the wide texture as before, filled from the left plane, the VRAM and
  the right plane. A mode switch of the dock recreates the context and the
  choice with it.
- `nx_vram_pack.c`: `dw3_nx_clamp_rect` and `dw3_nx_wide_rect_ok` (the clamp and
  the validity test the pack used, now shared; the wide test no longer overflows
  `int` for `dx` near `INT_MAX`), and `dw3_nx_copy16_rect` / `_wide`, tight
  halfword copies in the layouts of the two packs.
- **`DW3_PRESENT_16`** (`DW3_NX_TEST_ENV` on an nxlink build; there is no menu
  option): `0` the old CPU pack and RGBA8888 upload; `1` the halfwords go
  straight from VRAM with `GL_UNPACK_ROW_LENGTH`; `2` (the default) the rows are
  copied tight (`memcpy` per row) and uploaded in one piece. Mode 1 was the
  first form; in Ryubing the guest driver spent more time on a strided upload
  than the copy costs, so 2 is the default and 1 stays for the A/B.
- **`pace dbg:`** gains `p16=` (0, 1 or 2), `pack_us=` (of `present_us`: the
  RGBA8888 pack, or the tight copy of mode 2; 0 for mode 1) and `upload_us=` (of
  `present_us`: the `glTexSubImage2D` calls). A `present path:` line says which
  path was chosen, once, and why when it is the old one. `DW3_NX_OUT_HASH=1`
  (development only) reads the finished picture back and logs a hash per shown
  frame (`out hash: present=N frame=F WxH <hash>`), so two runs of one replay can
  be compared on the console's own driver.

There is no diff to explain: the PC picture does not go through `nx_gpu.c`, so the
two replay sweeps show that the shared files (`nx_vram_pack.*` is built only by
the Switch image and the host test) did not disturb the PC host; they are not
evidence about the Switch picture. The Switch picture is covered by the GPU test
and the Ryubing hashes above.

The first form of the new shader used `precision highp float` for the
coordinates and failed the scaled images (every third column at 3x, from column
719): llvmpipe runs `mediump` in fp16, so the **old** shader's texel choice was
the less exact one, and the new one picked the exact one. A GPU with fp32
`mediump` (the Switch's, as far as is known) gives no such difference, but
keeping the old precision for the coordinates makes the two paths agree on any
driver, so that is what shipped.

The 24-bit (movie) mode, the mask bit and the dither are not in the present:
the movie is converted to RGB555 by `__wrap_LoadImage` (`nx_present.c`) before
it reaches VRAM, the mask bit is kept in bit 15 and decides only the overlay's
black pixels (covered above), dither is the rasterizer's, and the title's edge
fade is the title overlay's own pass after the present. `nx_gpu.c`'s present has
no dither or brightness stage: it expands what VRAM holds. The all-values test
is therefore the whole colour contract of the present.

On this PC with Mesa llvmpipe (`present16_gpu_test --bench`, 320x240 shown at

that says nothing about a GPU.

`swap_us` and `busy_pct`, and the picture is 4.5x scaled when docked); whether
the console's `glTexSubImage2D` prefers mode 1 or mode 2 (`upload_us`); that
nouveau accepts `R16UI` (Ryubing's guest driver does; if the console's does not,
the log says `present path: RGBA8888 pack on the CPU (the 16-bit shaders or
R16UI textures failed on this driver)` and the old path runs). If the picture
looks different, `DW3_NX_OUT_HASH=1` in both builds says whether the pixels
differ. The battle mesh layer (`nx_mesh.c`) still packs its own RGBA8888 upload
of the dirty VRAM; that is a separate item and was not touched.

## Method

The PC host draws with SDL_GPU, so the Switch's rasterizer does not run there.
The profiler build (`-DDW3_PC_FIELD_PROF=ON`, `platforms/pc/src/field_prof.c`,
off by default) wraps the `Draw_*` contract at link time and feeds every call
to the PC renderer and to a shadow copy of `sdl3_software.c`, with the same
gutter bookkeeping as `switch_gpu.c`. Nothing the PC shows or the game does
changes: the replay captures of the profiler build match the references (222
checks, 0 failed). Every 60 game frames it prints a `dw3-prof:` line:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `cpu_us` | the game thread's CPU per frame (`CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID`) |
| `soft_us` | the shadow CPU rasterizer, per primitive kind (`tile`, `sprt`, `pf`, `pg`, `pft`, `pgt`, `line`, `img`: count / bounding-box kilopixels / us) |
| `pcgpu_us`, `vsync_cpu_us` | the PC's own renderer and present (PC only, not on the Switch) |
| `game_us` | the rest: decomp logic, OT walk and packet dispatch, pad, sound sequencer |
| `load_kb` | `LoadImage` bytes per frame (VRAM uploads) |
| `mix_us_per_s` | the SPU mixer's CPU per second of audio (its own thread) |

`DW3_PROF_SAMPLE=<file>` samples the game thread (SIGPROF on its CPU clock)
for a function-level split; `DW3_SOFT_TRACE=<file>` writes a binary trace of
every `Draw_*` call, and `DW3_SOFT_HASH=<file>` a hash of the shadow VRAM per
frame. `tools/soft_raster_bench.sh` replays a trace through the current
rasterizer and a reference revision built into the same binary, times both
per primitive kind and compares VRAM and the gutter planes after every frame
(`--fuzz N` does the same with random primitives). `perf` is not usable on this
desktop (`perf_event_paranoid` 4).

Runs: PC US and EU, Release (GCC 13, `-O3`), Xvfb, `DW3_TURBO=4`, driven by
replay recordings: the Forest Inn with `DW3_START_MODE=0x223 DW3_PROGRESS=6`
(stage WSTAG360) and a walk to the top and to the bottom of the room, and the
P6.15 scenarios. The desktop was shared with other jobs, so absolute times
move by tens of percent between runs; the bench interleaves old and new, so
its ratios are the stable numbers. Evidence (logs, tables, scripts,
screenshots; not committed):
`tmp/evidence/2026-10-24-p622-field-cpu/`.

## From the PC to the OLED

The OLED (Cortex-A57 at the stock 1020 MHz) is slower than this PC per
thread. One calibration point: the rc7 OLED log gives 13.7 ms per field frame,
of which about 0.7 ms is the present (2.8 ms per present, one present every
fourth frame at 4x). The PC's Switch-relevant part of the same kind of frames
(rasterizer plus game logic) is about 2.45 ms, so the OLED is about 5.3x the
PC here. The new rasterizer brings the field to 0.5-0.9 ms of rasterizer plus
logic on the PC, which at 5.3x is 3.2-5.3 ms per frame on the OLED with the
present: about 190-300 game fps, so at the 4x cap in most places. The A57 does not scale uniformly (the old
code was division- and branch-bound, the new code is mostly loads and
stores), so the doc's planning range is 160-240 fps at 4x, capped by the 4x
target. The OLED check is open: the same A/B in the Forest Inn and the New
Game opening with `frame dt us=` and `mesh dbg:` lines on a `--nxlink`
build, US and EU.

## What changed

PSY-Z `sdl3_software.c` (branch `dw3-soft-raster-fast` on the Larbaco fork,
`80fa492` and the review fix `2f931d8` on top of `ef67c4a`), the Switch's
renderer for everything outside battle:

- Rectangles: each row is clipped once into up to three runs (left gutter,
  VRAM, right gutter) instead of `ClipX0`/`ClipX1` and `PixelPtr` per pixel;
  the texture row, the CLUT row and `ShadeTexel`'s modulation (a table per
  channel) are looked up once per rectangle; no division per pixel unless the
  horizontal grid scales; an opaque untextured rectangle without mask test is
  a fill, and an opaque textured one at the neutral color without texture
  window or mask test copies texels with the mask bit.
- Triangles: the edge functions step by a constant per column; a row ends at
  the first miss after a hit (each edge test passes on a half-line); texture
  coordinates and Gouraud colors step with an exact quotient and remainder
  instead of three to five 64-bit divisions per pixel; the texture page is
  decoded once; flat neutral undithered texels skip `ShadeTexel`.
- Pixels, their order and their values are unchanged, including texture reads
  of areas the same primitive writes.
- Review fix (`2f931d8`): `80fa492` relied on the row clamps for `PixelPtr`'s
  y test, but clamping to the area and then to VRAM leaves row 511 when the
  area starts at y >= 512 (a 10-bit GP0 E3 y that libgpu never sends), so a
  triangle drew that row where the old code drew nothing. Triangle rows are
  now limited to the area too. The reviewer's repro (area (0,600)-(1023,1023),
  `GP0 20ffffff 00000000 0000012c 02bc0000`) writes 81 px with `80fa492`
  and 0 px now, as `ef67c4a`. Rectangles already test each row.
